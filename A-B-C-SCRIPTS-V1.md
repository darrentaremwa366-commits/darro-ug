There's a way most product businesses sell here in Africa today. You post a nice photo on Instagram. Someone DMs you for the price. You reply on WhatsApp. You go back and forth on size, color, delivery area, payment method. They agree. You pack. You deliver. You get paid.

---

So let's start with the Old Way. I'm going to draw this out.

The Old Way is built on conversations. Every sale is a new conversation between the owner or one employee and a customer. Let me walk you through a typical one.

A customer sees a product post on Instagram or TikTok. They go to your profile. They want price, they want size, they want delivery information. But it's not there. So they copy your WhatsApp number or send you a DM that says: price?

That's step one.

Step two: they wait. Maybe you're packing. Maybe you're in a bodaboda on a delivery. Maybe you're in a meeting. It's 9:30 pm and you'll reply in the morning.

Step three: reply and start the back-and-forth. Price? Size? Color? Delivery to where? Delivery fee? How to pay? Can I pay on delivery?

Step four: once that's all agreed, confirm the order manually. Write down the order. Maybe in a notebook. Maybe in a chat message. Save the customer address.

Step five: pack, dispatch, deliver, collect payment.

Six steps. Four of them depend entirely on a human being being available, thinking clearly, remembering to reply, remembering to record the order.

At 20 orders a week --- this works. You're busy but it's manageable.

At 80 orders a week --- you're always behind. Messages are missed. Customers wait three hours and order from someone else.

At 200 orders a week --- you need another person just to reply. Then another. And then another. Each new sale requires more humans to manage the conversations before any actual money comes in.

That's not a business scaling. That's a job scaling.

---

Now --- the Old Way has five structural ceilings that you cannot post your way out of.

Ceiling one: Reply speed is the bottleneck. Revenue depends on how fast one person or a few people can type. You can't reply to 300 messages a day and also pack orders, buy stock, do deliveries, and run the business. So revenue has a hard cap based on chat speed.

Ceiling two: You can't measure the leaks. You have no idea how many people saw your profile, copied your number, opened WhatsApp, typed a message, and left because you didn't reply in 20 minutes. You never see them. They are invisible. So you can't fix what you can't see.

Ceiling three: Information friction costs you orders. Every question --- price, size, color, delivery, payment, returns --- is a place where a customer can say "too much work" and disappear. They don't tell you this. They just don't reply. In the Old Way, every single customer has to ask every single question, manually, every single time.

Ceiling four: No repeat-purchase system. If someone buys today --- great. You know them for a week. In 60 days, if they don't message you, you have no way of knowing they exist. No list. No reminder. No new product announcement to them. A repeat customer has to start the entire conversation process all over again, just like a stranger.

Ceiling five: Decisions are memory-based, not data-based. "This product is selling well" means you remember packing a lot of it. "This week was slower" means it felt slower. There's no way to compare this month to last month, to know which product actually makes you the most profit, to know which post actually produced the orders.

Those are not temporary problems. They are the design of the Old Way. The Old Way is conversations. Conversations don't scale. They don't measure. They don't remember.

---

So what is the New Way? It's not throwing away Instagram or WhatsApp. Both of those are extremely powerful in African markets. You keep them. You just don't ask them to do jobs they were never built for.

Instagram and TikTok do one job very well: attention. They get people to see you. Great. Keep posting.

WhatsApp does one job very well: human conversation and order confirmation. Customers want to talk to a real person when a delivery goes wrong. Great. Keep WhatsApp.

But the jobs in between --- product information, product selection, order recording, customer lists, measurement, follow-up reminders --- those jobs are better handled by a system.

In the New Way:

A customer sees attention (post, Reel, referral). They are sent to somewhere they can see products, prices, sizes, delivery areas, payment methods, everything --- without talking to a human first. They select. They start an order. The completed order --- not a question --- routes to WhatsApp. Your job is no longer to answer 14 questions and negotiate an order. Your job is to confirm a ready-made order.

Then: every customer is recorded. Products, what they bought, how much they spent, when. When new stock comes in, there is a list of customers to contact, not just unread chats.

Then: numbers are visible. Sales per week. Which products sell. Which products get looked at and never purchased. Which traffic sources actually produce orders.

The owner no longer runs the business inside a WhatsApp chat list. The owner runs the business by looking at numbers, improving offers, fixing weak spots in the buying experience, and following up with customers.

This is not "having a website because websites look professional." That is a mistake many people make --- they build an online brochure, upload photos, add a contact form, and then wonder why nothing changed.

The New Way is about system design. Every stage of the customer journey has a job. The right tool does the job. WhatsApp does what WhatsApp is good at. Instagram does what Instagram is good at. A product site does the information-and-selection job. A dashboard does the measurement job.

That's it. Nothing fancy.

You don't know which posts actually produced inquires. You don't know which products were asked about most. You don't know your real inquiry-to-order conversion rate. Most owners believe that total sales revenue is the most important number. They look at how much they sold this month and use that to decide if the business is doing well. When you sell more, you also buy more stock, pack more orders, make more deliveries, and spend more time. All of those things cost money. Most people believe that more followers, more likes, and more views means more money. They think a big audience is the key to sales. But think about it this way. If you have 100,000 followers and only 2 out of every 100 of them actually buy something, that is 200 buyers. If you have 5,000 followers and 4 out of every 100 buy, that is also 200 buyers. When you believe your fastest-moving product is your best product, you restock it first, give it the best display, and push it hardest. It is your profit margin per product. What you sell a product for, minus what it costs you to buy or make it. That difference is your profit. And that profit is the ceiling for everything else in your business.

If your profit on a product is too low, you cannot afford to run offers, you cannot offer free delivery, you cannot spend on marketing. If it is healthy, you have room to move and grow.

Here is the problem. Most owners know their selling price but do not actually know their profit per product. They know what came in, but not what stuck. That is why so many businesses look busy and still feel broke. They are measuring revenue, not profit.

To find this number, take one product. Write down what you sell it for. Write down what it costs you to buy or make. The difference is your profit margin. Do that for every product. If you only do one thing from this entire video, do this one. It takes 2 minutes per product and it changes how you see your entire business.

Next. Conversion rate.

---

[CANVA TITLE: 2. Conversion Rate]

This next number can double your revenue from the exact same messages you already get this month.

It is your conversion rate. What percentage of the people who reach out to you actually pay.

Think about it. If you get 300 messages this month and 6 people order, your conversion rate is 2 percent. If you get the same 300 messages and 12 people order, that is 4 percent. Same messages. Same audience. Double the sales. No extra spending, no extra content. Just more of the people who already wanted your product actually paying.

For context, the average online store converts at about 1.4 percent. The best ones hit 3 to 4 percent. Most product businesses in Africa are sitting at 1 to 2 percent and do not even know it. That means for every 100 people who reach out, 98 walk away without buying. Fixing even a small part of why they leave can change your revenue without spending a single shilling more on marketing.

To find your number, count how many messages or inquiries you got last week. Count how many orders you got. Orders divided by messages. That is your conversion rate.

Third. Average spend per order.

---

[CANVA TITLE: 3. Average Spend Per Order]

This one is the fastest way to make more money from the exact same number of customers you already have.

It is how much the average customer spends each time they buy from you. Not per month, per order.

Here is why it matters. If you find a way to make each customer spend 20 percent more per order, that is 20 percent more revenue from the same person. No new customer. No new marketing. Just a different way of presenting what you sell.

For context, the average fashion brand gets about 80 dollars per order, which is roughly 300,000 shillings. But most African product businesses are sitting much lower and do not realize it.

To find yours, take your total sales last week and divide by the number of orders. That is your average. The next video in this series is all about how to move this number up, with real examples.

Number four. Repeat customer rate.

---

[CANVA TITLE: 4. Repeat Customer Rate]

This next number shows you something most owners never think about. It can give you a month where a quarter of your sales come in without you posting or paying for anything.

It is your repeat customer rate. How many of the people who bought from you this month had bought from you before.

Here is why this matters. A customer who already trusts you is the cheapest customer you will ever get. You do not need to find them. You do not need to convince them. You just need to treat them well enough to come back. On top of that, repeat buyers tend to spend more than first-time buyers because they already trust your quality.

If every sale this month came from a brand new person, you are running on a treadmill. Every month starts from zero. But if 25 percent or more of your sales come from people who already bought before, the business gets easier every month because a quarter of the work is already done.

To find this, look at your last month's orders. How many of those names do you recognize? If almost none, that is the problem. And just noticing it is where fixing it starts.

Fifth. Revenue per message.

---

[CANVA TITLE: 5. Revenue Per Message]

This one catches something that fools you into thinking you are winning when you are actually losing money.

It is a simple number. Your total revenue divided by your total messages or visits. One number that tells you if the whole system is working.

Here is why you need it. You can convince more people to buy, but if each one spends less, you can actually make less money overall. Each number on its own looks fine. More buyers. Smaller orders. Feels like progress. But together, you lost money. This number catches that because it combines everything into one figure.

If you made 2 million from 500 messages last week, your number is 4,000 per message. Next week you made 2.2 million from 700 messages. Your number is now about 3,100 per message. Revenue went up. But you are actually earning less from every person who reaches out. Without this number, you would think you are growing.

To find it, take your total revenue last week and divide by how many people reached out or visited. Write it down. Track it for a few weeks and you will see patterns you cannot see any other way.

Six. Where paying customers come from.

---

[CANVA TITLE: 6. Where Paying Customers Come From]

This one tells you exactly which platform to stop wasting your time on.

It is tracking where your paying customers actually come from. Not where your likes come from. Not where your views come from. Where your money comes from.

Here is an example. Instagram brings you 100 inquiries and 10 sales. TikTok brings you 50 inquiries and 20 sales. TikTok brought you double the customers with half the audience. But most owners look at the 100 inquiries on Instagram and think that is where they should focus. They double down on the platform that gets attention, not the one that gets orders.

This is the fastest way to stop wasting hours posting on platforms that look busy but produce no money.

To find this, for every new order, just ask one question. "How did you find us?" Write the answer next to the sale. Do that for a week and you will have a clear picture of where your real customers come from.

Last one. Number seven. Where customers drop off.

---

[CANVA TITLE: 7. Where Customers Drop Off]

This last one saves you from testing 10 random things and guessing which one worked. It tells you the one thing to fix.

It is about understanding where in your buying process most people give up. Think about the journey a customer goes through. They see your product. They ask about it. They decide to buy. They confirm. They pay. At which step do most people disappear?

If half the people who ask about a product actually commit to buy, but only a small fraction finish paying, then your payment step is the problem. Fix that. If most people view your product but very few actually ask about it, then your product presentation or your price is the problem. Fix that.

This one does not directly put money in your pocket. But it saves you the 20 hours a month of testing random changes that never move anything. It tells you exactly where to focus.

To find it, go through a customer's purchase in your own business, step by step. Where did you get bored or confused? Where would you have given up? That is probably where your customers are dropping off too.

---

Now. If you only remember one thing from this video, remember this. You do not start with all 7. You start with one.

Take a notebook. Or open a simple sheet. Four columns. Date. Total sales in shillings. Number of orders. Notes. That is all. Two minutes a day. After a few weeks you can already answer your conversion rate, your average spend per order, and where your customers come from. That is 3 more numbers than most product business owners can answer today. After a few months, add one more. Grow with the business.

Measurement is not about being a numbers person. It is about building a habit. Small habits compound.

First up. Product bundles.

[CANVA TITLE: 1. Product Bundles]

A bundle is when you take two or more products and sell them together as one package, usually at a price that works out better than buying each item on its own.

When you sell this way, customers end up buying more than one item in a single order, so each order is worth more. It is one of the fastest ways to grow revenue from the customers you already have.

The biggest Shopify sportswear brand in the world, Gymshark, uses this on a dedicated Bundles page on their website. "Buy 2, Get 20% Off." Customers buy 2 or more items when they would have bought 1.

I ran this with my own brand, Darro. I combined leftover World Cup jerseys with the new season kits as a bundle. Stock that was sitting in boxes moved. New stock sold alongside it. One transaction, two products.

---

Second. Quantity and tiered offers.

[CANVA TITLE: 2. Quantity and Tiered Offers]

The more a customer buys in one order, the better the price per item. Buy 2, save 15 percent. Buy 3, save 25 percent. The customer gets a better deal, you get a bigger order.

It works where customers genuinely buy more than one --- clothes, perfumes, skincare, accessories, school items. Same customer, bigger order, no extra work to bring them in.

---

Third. Free shipping thresholds.

[CANVA TITLE: 3. Free Shipping Thresholds]

You set a minimum order amount for free delivery. If a customer's cart is below that amount, they add a small item to reach it instead of paying for delivery.

Gymshark does this --- order above a set amount and delivery is free. Customers who came to buy one thing end up adding a small item so they do not pay for shipping.

For your business, set the threshold a bit above what your average order is now. Same customer, slightly bigger order, every time.

---

Number four. Checkout add-ons and upsells.

[CANVA TITLE: 4. Checkout Add-ons and Upsells]

A small extra item offered at the moment a customer is confirming their order. Socks with a jersey. Lip balm with face cream. Sticker pack with a notebook.

Gymshark does this on their cart page with a "Complete your look" section. You have the leggings in cart, and they show matching items at small step-up prices.

Not every order takes it. But the ones that do are extra profit, at no extra cost. One suggestion at the right moment.

---

Fifth. Entry and hero product pair.

[CANVA TITLE: 5. Entry and Hero Product Pair]

Two products working together. An entry product is a cheap, low-risk first purchase. It does not make you much money on its own. What it does is turn a stranger into a paying customer who now trusts you and is on your list.

Weeks or months later, that same person comes back and buys your hero product at full price. The hero carries the real margin. The entry product's job was to start the relationship.

Most successful online brands are built around a pair like this.

---

Last one. Number six. Genuine scarcity and limited drops.

[CANVA TITLE: 6. Genuine Scarcity and Limited Drops]

A drop is when you release a limited quantity of a product for a short time. Customers who were going to "buy next week" buy today because the stock is about to run out.

This does not increase how much each customer spends. It improves conversion during the drop window. Faster cash in the bank. But it is not a permanent structure, so it is last.

The one rule. Scarcity only works when it is real. Fake "only 2 left" on stock you have plenty of destroys trust. Trust is worth more than one extra week of sales.
