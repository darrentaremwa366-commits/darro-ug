Why Your 1-Product 1-Price Approach Is Costing You Money

### 2. Main outcome

### 5. Exact script

Welcome to the first lesson of African Revenue Systems. In this module we are building the offers that will help you make more revenue from the customers and traffic you already have. By the end of Module 1, you will have three real offers built for your actual business, with the math checked to make sure they are profitable.

In this first video, we're going to do three things. We're going to understand the difference between a product and an offer. We're going to classify every product in your business into one of four simple groups. And then we're going to look at a real example from my own clothing brand, Darro, so you can see what happens when offers work well.

Let's start by opening your worksheet. If you haven't already, go and download the Offer Builder Worksheet from the resources section. Got it open? Good.

First idea. Write this down if you're taking notes. A product is not an offer.

A product is the item --- the shirt, the perfume, the handbag, the electronics accessory, the food container. An offer is the entire structure around that product that gives the customer a reason to buy now, or buy more, or buy a combination.

Most product businesses operate on a 1-product 1-price model. Every product has one price. You post it. Someone asks. You quote the price. If they buy, great. If they don't, you try again with another product another day.

There is nothing wrong with this at the very beginning. It helps you start. But it has a cost. In a 1-price model, every customer who buys buys at one level. You have no way to help them buy more. You have no way to move old stock. You have no way to introduce new customers at a lower entry point.

That is what offers change. They give you options.

Before we build any offers, we first need to classify your existing products. Pause this video right now, open your worksheet, and write down a list of every product or product category you currently sell. Be practical. If you sell 40 different colors of the same jersey, group them as jerseys. Done?

Now let's classify each one. I want you to mark each product as one of four types. This is where we draw on the whiteboard together.

Type one --- HERO product. Every business should have one or two. The hero product is what you're known for. It has decent margins. It is what new customers usually want to buy first, and what brings in the right type of customer. Write HERO next to the one or two products that best describe your brand.

Type two --- ENTRY product. An entry product is a lower-priced, lower-risk way for a customer to try your brand for the first time. Not everything you sell needs to be expensive. A leather shoe brand can have a leather wallet as entry. A skincare brand can have a small body butter as entry. If you don't have a clear entry product yet, that's fine --- we'll mark it as a gap and come back to it in the next lesson. Write ENTRY or GAP next to the item.

Type three --- COMPLEMENTARY product. These are small items that go naturally with a hero product. Hoodie and sweatpants. Face cream and lip balm. A phone case and a screen protector. Complementary products are how you raise the average order value in the next lesson. Write COMPLEMENT next to those.

Type four --- DEAD or SLOW stock. This is product you've had for a while. It's been sitting. It's still good, but people are not asking for it anymore. Most owners try to pretend dead stock doesn't exist. We're going to use offers to turn it into revenue. Write DEAD or SLOW next to anything you have had for longer than two full drops or three months and it hasn't moved.

Pause the video. Take five minutes. Classify every product on your list. Classify each one as Hero, Entry, Complementary, or Dead. Be honest.

Resume when you're done.

Okay. You now have a classified product list. Let's look at a real example of this in action.

When I was running my own clothing brand Darro, I had two product groups. First, I had remaining World Cup jerseys that I had not been able to sell out. Those were DEAD stock. Second, I had the new 2026-2027 season kits coming in --- those were going to be the HERO products.

Instead of discounting the dead stock to try and get rid of it, I combined the two into a single offer. Buy one new season kit at full price, get one remaining World Cup jersey free. It was a buy-one-get-one structure --- but it wasn't a simple 50% discount.

The result: the new kits sold out almost completely, the dead World Cup stock was cleared instead of sitting on a shelf, and the average order value was higher than if I had just sold one jersey at a time. The same customers, the same traffic, the same Instagram --- a different offer structure --- produced more revenue and less dead stock.

That is what this product classification exercise is for. It shows you what to combine before you ever write a price tag.

One more thing before you finish this video. You'll notice the worksheet already has a space for each product's stock count, its cost to you, its individual selling price, and its margin. We'll fill those in during lesson 1.3 when we run the math. But if you already know the numbers, you can start filling them in now.

Alright. You now have a product list, each item classified as Hero, Entry, Complementary, or Dead stock. That is the foundation of every offer we're going to build.

Keep your worksheet open. In the next lesson we're going to turn that classification into five real offer types you can run next week, using the products you already have.

5 Offer Types You Can Run Next Week --- No Website Needed

### 5. Exact script

In the last lesson you classified every product in your business as Hero, Entry, Complementary, or Dead stock.

In this lesson we're going to match those products to five practical offer types. We're going to do it together for a sample business, and then you're going to select three offers you can actually run for your own business.

Have your worksheet open. We're filling in the "Offer Type Selected" row next to each product group.

The five offer types we're building today do not require a website. They do not require paid advertising. They can run through Stories, WhatsApp broadcasts, WhatsApp group messages, saved replies, a poster in a physical shop, or all five.

Offer type one --- BUNDLES.

A bundle takes two or more products and sells them together at a single combined price that is a better value than buying them separately. The ideal bundle combines a Hero product with something else. Remember the classification chart we just built? Bundles work especially well when you combine Hero plus Dead stock (like my BOGO example), or Hero plus Complementary.

Example for a clothing brand: Hoodie alone 120k. Sweatpants alone 100k. Separately that is 220k. Bundle price 190k. Customer saves 30k. Business makes 190k instead of 120k from the person who only wanted a hoodie.

Example for a cosmetics brand: Face cream 80k, serum 70k, toner 60k. Full routine bundle: 180k instead of 210k. Routine sells together.

Pause the video. Look at your product list. Do you have a Hero + Complementary or Hero + Dead combination that becomes a bundle? Write "Bundle" next to it in the worksheet. If you are not sure yet, write "Maybe Bundle."

Resume when done.

Offer type two --- COMPLEMENTARY ADD-ONS.

An add-on is a small extra item offered right at the moment the customer is already ready to buy. Small price. Obvious match. Almost no extra sales work.

Clothing example: customer orders the hoodie at 120k. Reply says: "I can also include the matching cap for an extra 25k, it usually sells for 35k if you buy it alone." Customer is already buying. Yes or no is easy.

Perfume example: customer orders one perfume at 90k. Reply adds: "If you take the body spray with it, add 20k instead of the usual 40k."

Electronics example: earbuds 130k. "Add the matching case and cable for 20k more."

The add-on only works if it is: cheap compared to the main order, obviously related, and offered right at the point of purchase. It is not a separate sale. It is a yes-or-no extra with the existing sale.

Pause again. Look at your Complementary products. Can any of them become add-ons to a Hero product? Write "Add-on target" next to the complementary product, and "Add-on offer" next to the hero.

Resume.

Offer type three --- QUANTITY THRESHOLD OFFERS.

Buy two, save X. Buy three, save Y. Family packs. Multi-buys.

Example: children's school clothing. "Buy 3 items or more --- automatic 50k off." The customer who was going to buy two shirts now looks for a third item, or brings their cousin along too.

Example: food containers. Single container 18k. Set of three 45k. Effective per-unit price is 15k. Customer saves 9k overall. Business sells three instead of one.

Important rule: the threshold should genuinely match how people actually use the product. School clothes --- three makes sense. A 60-inch television --- a quantity threshold makes no sense. Match the offer to the product.

Offer type four --- ENTRY PRODUCTS.

An entry product solves the "I'm not sure I trust this brand yet" problem. It is a smaller, cheaper, lower-risk version of what you sell.

If you don't have a clear entry product yet, this is where you make one. It does not have to be a new product you manufacture. It can be a small selection, a sample size, a small accessory from your existing range that matches the hero.

Shoe brand example: hero is leather shoes at 250k. Entry product is a matching leather wallet at 60k. Someone who buys the wallet and trusts you is a future shoe buyer.

Cosmetics brand example: hero is the full face set at 150k. Entry product is a single lip butter at 25k. Low risk.

If you have an entry product gap from the last lesson, write "Entry product offer" next to the product you are going to use as entry.

Offer type five --- GENUINE SCARCITY AND LIMITED DROPS.

Scarcity works when it is true. You have 10 pieces of a small batch. You post: "This drop is 10 Ankara bags. When they are gone, the next drop is 4 weeks away." That is genuine scarcity. It gives a customer a reason to act now instead of thinking about it for a week.

Scarcity also works for dead stock in small quantities. "Final 5 jerseys from the World Cup drop. After these, gone forever. Price adjusted to move them." One clear message.

Scarcity does NOT work when it is fake. "Only 2 left" on stock you have 300 of. Your customers will notice. Trust is more valuable than a quick sale.

Alright. Five offer types. Your worksheet should already have offers written next to some of your products.

Now I want you to do one more exercise before this video ends. I want you to pick exactly THREE offers from the five types above that you are going to test in the next 90 days.

Write these three into your worksheet now. Offer one, offer two, offer three. Be specific. Not "bundle" but "hoodie + sweatpants bundle at 190k." Not "add-on" but "cap add-on to any jersey order for +25k."

If you get stuck, here is the simplest combination that works for almost every business: one bundle, one add-on, one limited drop or entry product depending on whether you have existing stock or a missing entry product.

Pick your three. Pause now. Resume when done.

Done? Great. We have the three offers selected. We have not done the math yet to make sure they are profitable. That is exactly what we do in the next lesson.

## D1.3 --- How to Build Your First 3 Offers Today (And Know They'll Be Profitable)

### 1. Video title
How to Build Your First 3 Offers Today (And Know They'll Be Profitable)

### 2. Main outcome
By the end, the student has calculated the per-unit and per-offer profit on each of their 3 selected offers, selected a promotion channel and test date, and is ready to launch one of them in Week 1.

### 3. Recommended length
10 to 12 minutes.

### 4. What to show on the whiteboard or Miro
- Margin math diagram on the board: Offer Sell Price minus Cost of Products in Offer minus Small Direct Costs (delivery if included, mobile money fees) = Profit Per Offer Sold.
- A live filled-in example for a sample business: Hoodie-Sweatpants Bundle --- cost 80k + 70k = 150k, sell at 190k, minus 5k delivery included = profit 35k per bundle sold.
- Promotion channel matrix: Stories (visual, fast), IG Saved Replies (first contact), WA broadcasts (customers list), WA Group (warm audience), in-shop poster (walk-ins).
- Promotion schedule area: Week 1 --- first offer launch date, Week 5 --- second, Week 10 --- third (matches the 90-day roadmap).

### 5. Exact script

You now have three specific offers selected for your business. Congratulations. Before we run any of them, we need to do the math.

The reason most business owners fail with offers is that they build something that sounds like a deal, sells a lot, and then at the end of the month they realize they actually lost money on every sale. That is not a good offer. That is a discount giveaway.

In this lesson we are calculating profit per offer. We're then going to choose a promotion channel for each one, and schedule them onto the 90-day plan. At the end of this lesson, offer number one is ready to launch.

Open the worksheet. Go to the section labeled Margin Per Offer. Got it? Good.

Let me show you the formula first, and then we're going to fill it in together for a sample.

Offer sell price --- that is the total amount the customer pays for the offer.
Minus cost of all products included --- if the bundle has two items, add the two cost-per-unit numbers together.
Minus any small direct costs that are part of the offer --- if you're including delivery in the price as a bonus, subtract the average delivery cost. If mobile money fees are a noticeable amount, subtract a rough percentage.
What's left equals profit per offer sold.

If this number is less than your normal profit on a hero product by itself --- something is wrong with the offer. You should earn more profit on the bundle or the add-on or the quantity offer than you would on just selling one hero product alone. Otherwise the extra complication is not worth it.

Let me walk you through an example. I'll use the Hoodie and Sweatpants Bundle from the last lesson.

Hoodie sells individually: 120k. Hoodie cost: 80k. Individual profit: 40k.
Sweatpants sells individually: 100k. Sweatpants cost: 70k. Individual profit: 30k.
Bundle offer price: 190k.
Total cost of bundle: 80k + 70k = 150k.
I'm going to include delivery within Kampala in the bundle price as a sweetener, average cost 5k.
So: 190k minus 150k minus 5k = 35k profit per bundle.

Wait. 35k profit on the bundle compared to 40k profit on one hoodie alone. That is actually less per-sale profit if the customer was already going to buy just the hoodie. So why do it?

Because the bundle increases the chance that the customer buys more in total, and also moves two products instead of one. If the business was going to sell one hoodie at 40k profit, and now sells the bundle at 35k profit but with an additional product out the door and 70k extra revenue for cash flow, the bundle still makes sense --- especially if one of the items is slow stock.

But if instead the profit worked out to only 10k per bundle, that would be too thin. You need to leave enough margin to cover your time and effort. There is no exact rule. Use your judgment. If the offer profit is lower than a single hero product profit by more than half, something needs adjusting. Either the offer price goes up a little, or you take the delivery cost out.

Now. Pause this video. Do this same math for each of your three offers in your worksheet. Be honest about your cost. If you're not sure of exact cost, round UP --- never round down. It is much better to think you will make 25k profit per offer and actually make 30k than the other way around.

Calculate for Offer 1, Offer 2, Offer 3. Resume when you have profit numbers for all three.

Good. Now look at each number.

If any offer has a profit you are not comfortable with --- do not scrap it. Adjust the price by 10 or 15 thousand. Or remove one included cost (for example, don't include free delivery, just make delivery separate and stated clearly). Adjust until the number feels okay.

Now we're going to pick one promotion channel and a date for each offer.

You have five simple promotion channels available right now. No website needed.

One --- Instagram Stories. Stories last 24 hours. They're perfect for offers with a time limit. Photo of the products, price of the offer, simple text. Add a link sticker if you have 10k+ followers, or just tell people to send a message with OFFER in the text.

Two --- Instagram Saved Replies or WhatsApp Quick Replies. When someone asks about a product, the reply includes the offer. No extra work.

Three --- WhatsApp Broadcast Lists. If you have a customer list (which we're building in Module 3), you send the offer to the right category of customers. This is the most powerful channel because these are people who already bought from you.

Four --- WhatsApp Customer Group, if you run one. For members, even a small exclusive extra (group members get free delivery on the bundle) will work very well.

Five --- in-shop physical poster. If you have a physical shop, one A4 poster near the counter shows the offer to every person who walks in.

For each of your three offers, pick one primary promotion channel. You can use more than one, but start with one primary so you can actually measure which one works best.

Then schedule the three launches across the next 90 days. The simplest approach: Launch Offer One in Week 1, Offer Two in Week 5, Offer Three in Week 10. That gives each offer 4-5 weeks to run, and after every cycle you can review what happened.

Write the channel and date for each offer into your worksheet. Pause and do that now.

Resume when done.

Okay. You now have three offers, with the math checked, profit per sale calculated, promotion channel selected, and launch dates. The entire build-together Offers section of the worksheet is complete.

That means Offer One is ready to launch next week. That is your first action from the entire course. You don't need to wait for any other module.

One final thing before you go. In the worksheet there is a copy-paste AI prompt. It asks you to type in your business, products, customers, which offer you want to promote, and any goals you have. Paste it into any AI tool and it will give you specific, tailored ideas for Stories, posts, and WA broadcasts for that exact offer --- not generic "post three times a day" advice. Use it to save time creating promotion content.

You have completed Module 1. Offers are built. In the next module we improve the buying experience so that when people see these new offers, they actually convert at a higher rate.

## D2.1 --- Why Customers Ask for the Price Then Disappear

### 1. Video title
Why Customers Ask for the Price Then Disappear

### 2. Main outcome
The student can map the 5-step interest-to-payment journey for their own business and identify at which step(s) they are currently losing the most people.

### 3. Recommended length
7 to 9 minutes.

### 4. What to show on the whiteboard or Miro
- A horizontal 5-step journey: Step 1 Interest on social or shop, Step 2 Info Search (price, sizes, delivery), Step 3 Contact (DM, call, visit), Step 4 Negotiation / Confirmation, Step 5 Payment + Delivery.
- Between each step, RED DROP ARROWS showing where people can exit.
- Below each step, an example of friction: Step 2 no prices anywhere, Step 3 3-hour reply delay, Step 4 8-message back-and-forth, Step 5 no clear payment instructions.
- A second copy of the journey, drawn alongside, showing a version with reduced friction --- info already available, fast reply template, checkout cut to 2 steps.

### 5. Exact script

In Module 1 we built offers. The goal was to make each customer worth more.

In Module 2 we're fixing the buying experience. The goal is to make more of the people who are already interested actually become customers, instead of disappearing.

In this first lesson we're going to map out the journey from the moment someone finds your business to the moment they pay. Then we're going to mark everywhere people can disappear. When you see your own map, you'll understand exactly why "price?" messages go quiet.

Grab a blank page of your notebook or open the Buying Experience Checklist PDF. We're drawing this together.

First, let me show you the default journey that almost every Instagram/WhatsApp/shop business follows. We'll call this the Old Journey.

Step one. Interest. A customer sees a post, a Story, a TikTok, a referral, or walks into your physical shop. Good. They are interested.

Step two. Information search. They want to know price, size, color options, delivery areas, delivery fees, payment methods, returns.

In the Old Journey, this information is not available in advance. So the customer now has to make a decision: am I willing to contact the owner and ask these questions, and then wait for the reply?

Most people are not willing. They leave right here. The DROP ARROW at step two is the biggest and most expensive one in most businesses.

Step three. Contact. The customer decides to ask. They send a DM, they copy the WhatsApp number, they walk up to the counter, or they call.

Now they wait for a reply. If the reply is fast --- good. If the reply takes 20 minutes, 2 hours, or until tomorrow --- another drop arrow. Many customers disappear right here because they found someone else who answered faster, or simply changed their mind during the wait.

Step four. Confirmation and back-and-forth. Price is 150k? What sizes? Do you deliver to Jinja? How much is delivery? Can I pay on delivery? Can I see a photo of the back? Each question is a small decision. Each decision point is another drop arrow. Customers disappear one question at a time.

Step five. Payment and delivery. Finally they say yes. Now you need address, payment method, mobile money confirmation, delivery timing. If this step is unclear or takes too much more effort, some people even disappear here.

Okay. That is five steps. Four of those steps depend entirely on how fast a human can type, how well they can remember information, and how available they are at that exact moment.

Now let's draw this for YOUR business.

Pause this video. Draw five steps on a page or fill in the five steps on the checklist PDF. For each step, write down what a customer actually does today. Be honest. If price is NEVER shown in Stories or highlights, write "customer has to ask for price." If the customer has to message you to get a delivery area list, write "customer has to ask delivery areas." If payment method has to be negotiated every time, write "customer has to ask payment."

Resume when you've mapped all five steps.

Good. Now look at your map and put a circle around the steps that have the most "has to ask" items. Each "has to ask" is a place a person can decide not to.

This is why customers ask for the price then disappear. It is almost never because the price was too high. It is because after asking the price, they then have to ask size. Then they have to ask delivery area. Then they have to ask how to pay. Then they wait. By the time you get to the fourth question, they have mentally checked out. It was not the price. It was the effort.

That is the key insight. If your customer has to ask four or five basic questions before they can even decide, your conversion will always be low, no matter how good your products or your prices are.

In the next two lessons we're going to fix this. Lesson 2.2 is the fast five-minute information fix. Lesson 2.3 is about replying faster and cutting the number of checkout steps in half.

### 6. Real-world examples
- A real (anonymized) WA chat transcript: customer asks price, 45 min wait, asks sizes, wait, asks delivery, wait --- at message 5 the customer never replies again.
- The Darro example: early on, every order had 6+ messages; after implementing the fixes in the next two lessons, it was down to 2-3 messages per confirmed order.

## D2.2 --- The 5-Minute Information Fix That Reduces Messages by Half

### 1. Video title
The 5-Minute Information Fix That Reduces Messages by Half

### 2. Main outcome
The student has audited the top 5 customer questions, written a reusable first-reply message that answers all 5, and placed the same information in the easiest-to-find spot for the channel.

### 3. Recommended length
6 to 8 minutes.

### 4. What to show on the whiteboard or Miro
- Top 5 questions list, each with a short answer example written next to it.
- A finished Standard First Reply template on the board (a single WA message block): greeting, thank, product list or catalog note, price ranges, sizes, delivery areas, payment methods, how to order --- all in one formatted message.
- Three places to publish: (1) WA Quick Reply or IG Saved Reply, (2) Instagram Stories Highlights (Pinned Stories: PRICES, SIZES, DELIVERY, PAYMENT, ORDER), (3) a physical shop info sheet or counter poster.

### 5. Exact script

You have just mapped your five-step journey, and you have probably circled three or four places where the customer has to ask a basic question just to get information that should already be available.

In this lesson we are doing the five-minute fix. It is the highest return on time investment in the entire course. Five minutes of work today can literally cut your incoming message volume by half, which frees you up to reply faster to the people who are serious.

We're going to do three small things. One, find the top five questions. Two, write one reusable message that answers all five. Three, put that information somewhere easy to find before the customer has to ask.

Let's start with the top five questions.

From your own experience answering customers, what are the five things people ask most often? Let me guess the most common ones in African product businesses.

One. How much is it? Price.
Two. What sizes, colors, or options do you have?
Three. Do you deliver to my area? Which areas? How much is delivery?
Four. How do I pay? Mobile money? Cash on delivery? Bank?
Five. How do I actually order? What are the steps?

Most businesses get more than 80% of their questions from just these five categories. Think about the last 20 messages you received. Count how many were one of these five.

Pause the video. Open the Buying Experience Checklist. In the box labeled Top 5 Questions, write the exact five questions your customers ask you most. If they ask a question not on my list --- for example, "is this original?" for electronics or sneakers --- put that real question instead of one of mine. Use your own.

Resume when you have five.

Now we are going to write the Standard First Reply. This is a single message that answers all five questions clearly. You are going to save this message once, and then use it for every single new customer who makes a first inquiry.

Here is the structure. Keep it friendly. One paragraph or bullet points inside WhatsApp, not a formal letter.

Start with a simple greeting: "Hey, thanks for your message. Here's the quick info you're looking for."

Then answer each of the five questions you wrote.

One --- Prices: "Prices for the main jersey range are 180k to 220k depending on the style. Cap add-ons are 30k."

Two --- Options: "All jerseys come in sizes S to 3XL. All colors are shown in the pinned highlights on my profile or the photo album here."

Three --- Delivery: "I deliver within Kampala at 10k. Outside Kampala to Wakiso, Entebbe, Mukono at 15k-25k depending on location. Delivery times are 1-2 working days."

Four --- Payment: "Payment is by mobile money to 07XX XXX XXX. Cash on delivery also available for an extra 5k fee."

Five --- How to order: "To order, just send the product photo or name, size, and delivery address. I confirm the total and arrange dispatch."

That's it. One message. Five questions answered. No more back-and-forth asking every single one.

Pause the video. Write your Standard First Reply in the checklist using your own five questions and your own business details. Make it sound like you. Don't copy a template verbatim if it doesn't sound natural. Make it yours.

Resume when done.

Now we're going to put this information in three places so customers find it BEFORE they have to message you.

Place one. Save your Standard First Reply as a WhatsApp Business Quick Reply if you use WhatsApp Business, or as a saved message in your phone's notes or pinned chat if you don't. When a new customer messages, one tap sends everything.

Place two. Put the five answers in your Instagram or TikTok Stories Highlights. Create five separate highlights with simple names: PRICES, SIZES, DELIVERY, PAYMENT, HOW TO ORDER. Each highlight is a 3-slide Story that answers that one question. Pin them to the top of your profile so every profile visitor sees them before deciding whether to message.

Place three. If you have a physical shop, print the five answers on a single A4 sheet and stick it near the counter or in the window. Walk-in customers will glance at it, know prices and options, and ask fewer basic questions.

That is the entire fix. One Standard Reply, three places. It takes five minutes to do. It reduces first-time messages by half. It converts more of the people who do message into orders.

One warning. Some owners are afraid to put prices on Stories or counters because they think customers won't message. The opposite happens. Price is the number-one filter. If your price is way out of a person's budget, they shouldn't message you anyway --- you don't want to spend 30 minutes on a conversation that ends with "too expensive." If your price is right for them, they message ready to buy with fewer questions. Putting prices and information in front of people saves everyone time.

## D3.1 --- Why Every Paying Customer Is Worth More Than One Sale

### 1. Video title
Why Every Paying Customer Is Worth More Than One Sale

### 2. Main outcome
The student understands the economic difference between always chasing new customers vs. bringing back existing ones. They also grasp why a simple customer list and a WA broadcast channel is the most valuable asset in the business.

### 3. Recommended length
7 to 9 minutes.

### 4. What to show on the whiteboard or Miro
- Simple repeat-customer economics drawn as two columns: NEW CUSTOMER vs OLD CUSTOMER. New customer cost: content, time, trust building, 1-2% to 5% inquiry conversion. Old customer cost: one WA broadcast, trust already exists, 10% to 30% reorder rate is realistic.
- A diagram of the Darro WhatsApp group journey: giveaway attracted N new interested members -> some bought in the next jersey drop -> members have exclusive discount -> direct WA broadcast sales reach without paid ads.
- The most valuable asset ranking: 1. Customer list (WA numbers), 2. Past order + offer data, 3. Instagram followers, 4. Nice photos. (Written as a simple list, emphasis on WA list being first.)

### 5. Exact script

You built better offers in Module 1. You fixed the buying experience in Module 2. Now we're working on the cheapest, most under-utilized revenue lever in almost every product business: the customers you already have.

The goal of Module 3 is to build a simple customer system that helps you sell more to the people who already bought from you, without chasing new customers every single week.

This lesson is the "why." Lessons 2 and 3 of Module 3 are the "how."

Let me start with simple economics. No jargon.

If I want to get a new customer to buy from my product business, what do I have to do?

I have to create content. Post it. Hope someone sees it. They look at my profile. If they're interested, they message. I answer questions. They maybe order. On average this works 1 time in 50 or 1 in 100 inquiries depending on the business. It takes time. It takes posting. It takes trust building every single time.

Compare that to selling to a customer who has already bought from me once.

They already know my products. They know delivery works. They trust the payment method. I have their WhatsApp number. If I send them a message about a new drop or a bundle offer that fits what they bought before, the chance they buy again is 10%, 20%, even 30% depending on the business. And the work to do it --- one broadcast message.

That is the economics. Existing customers are dramatically easier to sell to again because trust already exists.

Most owners, though, treat every sale like a one-off transaction. Money comes in. Product goes out. Conversation ends. 60 days later, that customer is just another random person scrolling Instagram.

That is a huge waste. You already did the hardest work to get them to buy once. Why throw away the relationship after?

Now, let me show you a real example of how this works using my own Darro WhatsApp group.

Early on I ran a giveaway. I announced a few jerseys as the prize. To enter, people had to join the customer WhatsApp group.

The giveaway attracted many more new interested members than the number of jerseys I actually gave away. That's expected --- giveaways attract the right audience when the prize is a product you actually sell, not a random phone or free airtime.

The group then had several effects. First: group members got a slightly better discount than the public, which gave them a reason to stay and buy inside the group. Second: when a new jersey drop came, the group members were the first to know and the first to buy. I can directly trace a large share of the sold-out BOGO drop to group members who had previously entered the giveaway and never bought, then purchased in the next drop because they were still in the group and still seeing my offers.

Third: the group is a direct broadcast channel. I don't have to depend on Instagram or Meta algorithms showing posts to people. If I have a new drop, a restock, or an offer, I write one message, send it to the group or a broadcast list, and every member sees it. That reach is worth more than any number of followers on Instagram, because followers may or may not see the post.

That is what a simple customer system gives you. Owned, direct access to people who already like your products. It is the single most valuable asset in a product business. More valuable than followers. More valuable than nice photos. More valuable than almost anything else.

Now --- I'm not saying every business needs a WA group. Some businesses don't have enough customers yet, or a group might not fit their customers' style. The foundation, even without a group, is the same thing: a simple customer list --- name, number, what they bought, when. That list is the business. Everything else sits on top of it.

In the next lesson, we are building that list. We'll categorize customers into three groups so you know who to message and when. We'll cover whether to start a customer group and, if yes, how to launch it with a small giveaway exactly like the one I ran for Darro.

### 5. Exact script

You now understand why past customers matter. In this lesson we're building the actual system. We're doing three things: building the customer list, categorizing each customer, and then deciding whether a WA group makes sense for your business right now.

Open the Customer List Template spreadsheet or the printable page. We are going to fill it in together.

The list has seven columns. Name. WhatsApp number. What they bought. Date they bought. Amount spent. Which category. Last contact date. Notes.

That's all. No CRM. No tags. No software. Just seven columns.

The important question is: where do you get the list from?

Four places.

One. Your WhatsApp chats. Scroll through chats from the last 3-6 months. Every chat where you can see a completed order --- write down their name, number, what they bought, date, amount. This is the fastest source for most businesses.

Two. Receipt books or paper order books. If you write physical receipts, go through them and type or write in the same columns.

Three. Delivery waybills or delivery books. Every delivery record is also a customer record.

Four. In-shop walk-in notebook if you keep one.

If you have hundreds of customers, you do NOT have to type them all in one sitting. Start with the last 3 months. Add older customers later, 20 a night, while you are watching TV. Done over a week it doesn't feel like work.

Pause the video. Start filling in customers. Add 50 rows minimum. Or if you have fewer than 50 past customers, add everyone you have. Resume when you have the list started.

Good. Now the second step: categorization. Every customer on the list goes into one of three boxes. We are sorting by how recently they bought.

Category one --- RECENT. Bought in the last 30 days.

Category two --- 30 to 90 DAYS AGO. Bought between one and three months ago.

Category three --- 90+ DAYS AGO. Bought more than three months ago.

That's all. No 12-category system. Just three.

Why do we categorize? Because the message you send to a customer who bought last week should be different from the message you send to a customer who bought 9 months ago.

Recent customers --- message soon, but not pushy. A thank-you message. A gentle follow-up a week later: "Hope the product arrived well. If you need anything, I'm here." Then, in 3-4 weeks, a new product or new drop notice.

30 to 90 days --- this is your warm market. A monthly offer broadcast, a restock announcement, a new drop message. One per week maximum. More than that becomes spam.

90+ days --- these are your lapsed or cold customers. Message them only when there is a genuine reason: a real new drop, a reactivation offer, or a group giveaway launch. Don't hit them with weekly promotions.

Pause the video again. For every customer on your list, write one of the three categories in the category column. Then also fill in the last contact date (the date of their last order or last message). Resume when done.

Now, step three. The Customer Group question.

A WhatsApp customer group is a powerful tool --- but not every business needs one right now.

The simple rule. Do you have 50 or more real past customers on your list? If no, don't start a group yet. Use WhatsApp Broadcast Lists instead. Broadcast lists allow you to send a single message to up to 256 numbers at a time, privately, without a group chat. This is simpler and works perfectly up to a few hundred customers.

If yes --- you have 50+ customers and you want closer relationships with them --- here is the playbook for starting.

One. Run a small giveaway with your actual product as the prize. NOT airtime. NOT a phone. A product you actually sell. For a clothing brand it is one or two jerseys. For cosmetics it is a product bundle. For food it is a family pack. The prize must attract people who want to buy from you, not people who want any free thing.

Two. Announce the giveaway to all existing customers via a broadcast. Tell them: "I'm starting a private customer group. Join to enter the giveaway. Group members also get a small exclusive discount on every order and are the first to know about new drops." That combination --- giveaway + discount + early access --- works very well.

Three. First week in the group. Welcome message. Set 3-4 simple rules (no ads from other members, no insults, English + local language welcome, be polite). Then introduce the three group benefits clearly: early drops, exclusive discount, direct access to you. Post one real offer or one behind-the-scenes post in the first 48 hours so it is not all giveaway and no value.

Four. Posting frequency after launch: 2 to 4 posts a week max. Not daily product spam. Mix it up. Offer once, behind-the-scenes once, customer photo once, new stock notice once. If every post is "buy buy buy," people mute the group.

That is the playbook. You do NOT need 1,000 people in a group to make it work. Even 70 real buying customers in a group is a direct broadcast channel worth a fortune.

Pause. Decide. Are you starting a group now, or starting with Broadcast Lists? If group, write the giveaway product and launch date into your worksheet. If lists only, note that.

Resume.

### 5. Exact script

Module 4 is the smallest module in the course. Two videos. Only one simple spreadsheet tab. Only one habit.

But it is the most important habit long-term. If you do not do anything else from this course, do this one thing. It will change how your business grows forever.

The simple idea is: business money and personal money must live in two separate places.

Almost every small product business in Africa eventually hits the same wall. The owner sells a lot. Money comes in. The money is used for food, rent, school fees, family emergencies, and life. Then, when it is time to buy the next batch of stock, the money is not there. The business was busy. The business looked successful. The owner feels like they worked hard. But they cannot re-order. The business stalls or shrinks, not because of bad products or bad sales, but because money was managed as one pot.

I've done this myself early on with Darro. After a successful drop, the account feels full. It feels like profit. Life expenses happen. A few weeks later I want to re-order the next drop and I realize there is not enough money to buy the same quantity again. That is the exact moment I started separating the two.

It sounds simple. But very few owners actually do it consistently.

So here is the rule, and it works even if you do not have a business bank account. You don't need a bank account at all to start.

The Two Wallet Rule.

If you use mobile money, get two separate SIMs, or use two different mobile money accounts if one provider allows it. One phone number = Business account. One phone number = Personal account.

Or if you have one SIM, two physical wallets, envelopes, or even two separate drawers. One labeled BUSINESS. One PERSONAL.

Every single naira, shilling, or rand that comes in from a sale goes into the BUSINESS side first. Not personal. Business.

Then, from the business side, ONLY the following things are paid:

- Product stock re-orders
- Delivery costs, bodaboda, courier
- Airtime and mobile money fees directly related to the business
- Any direct business expense

What does NOT come out of business money first?

- Your house rent
- Your food
- School fees
- Any personal expense
- Random family support

Personal expenses are paid ONLY from a decided amount that moves, on purpose, from the business pot to the personal pot --- a weekly or monthly "owner pay" number you decide.

And whatever is left in the business pot after stock and costs and owner pay, stays in the business for the next drop.

That is the entire system.

I'm going to show you how this looks with a simple example, and then you can do the same exercise with your last big sale.

Suppose a good week. 20 orders at 200k average = 4,000,000 in sales.

The stock for those 20 orders cost you 2,400,000.
Delivery for the week: 180,000.
Airtime and fees: 20,000.
Direct business costs total: 2,600,000.

Money left after costs: 1,400,000.

Now, you decide an owner pay for yourself. Not 1,400,000. Not what feels good. A sensible amount. Maybe 600,000 this week. Move it to personal.

Remaining in business pot: 800,000.

That 800,000 then goes toward the next stock order, plus buffer. So next week you can buy 800k more stock than last time. That is growth.

Compare this to the one-pot version: 4 million comes in. You feel rich. You take 2 million for life expenses. 1.5 million for various costs. Next stock order comes and you only have 500k left. You buy one third of the stock you need. The business shrinks instead of growing, even though you had "good sales."

That is why this is the most important habit in the course. It is the difference between a business that compounds and a job where the money disappears.

Your action for this video: by the end of today, decide how you are going to split things. Two mobile money accounts? Two wallets? Two drawers? Two envelopes? Pick the method that fits the tools you already have. Don't wait for a bank account. Do it today.

In the next and final numbers video we're going to track just three numbers every week, in one simple sheet, so you can see progress.

## D4.2 --- 3 Numbers I Would Track Every Week If I Ran Your Business

### 1. Video title
3 Numbers I Would Track Every Week If I Ran Your Business

### 5. Exact script

You have now separated business money from personal money. That was the discipline. Now we need to see if anything is actually working.

We are going to track exactly three numbers every week. That's it. Not 7. Not 20. Three. In one single spreadsheet tab or a notebook page. It takes two minutes on Sunday night.

The three numbers are:

One. Total sales for the week --- in shillings or whatever your currency.
Two. Number of orders in the week.
Three. Expenses directly related to that week --- stock bought, delivery, airtime/fees, other business costs.

From those three numbers we can also automatically see a fourth number that is very useful: average order value, which is total sales divided by number of orders.

Now, if you already tried tracking more than three numbers before and quit --- you are not alone. Seven, ten, 20 numbers is too much for the first habit. Three is the maximum that a real business owner will actually do every Sunday night without quitting.

Let's fill in the tracker together for a sample week. I'm going to write the numbers into the tracker on the board, and if you already have last week's numbers, pause and enter yours at the same time.

Sample week: Monday to Sunday.
Total sales (money received): 4,200,000
Number of orders: 21
Offer run this week: Hoodie + Sweatpants Bundle
Direct expenses this week:
- Stock bought for next drop: 1,500,000
- Delivery bodaboda: 160,000
- Airtime and fees: 25,000
Expenses total: 1,685,000

Average order value: 4,200,000 divided by 21 = exactly 200,000. That is useful. Last week it was 170,000. So the average order value is up because the bundle offer is working.

Profit this week is calculated roughly as: total sales minus direct expenses. In this sample that's 4,200,000 minus 1,685,000 = 2,515,000. But wait --- if some of the stock bought this week is for NEXT week, the real profit is not exactly the subtraction. That is fine for a small business. What we care about week to week is the trend. If for four weeks the simple profit number keeps going up, the business is growing. If it goes sideways for 8 weeks, something needs changing.

Then the notes section. I write anything unusual. "New offer ran this week. Most orders were bundle. Instagram Story was the main source. Also one big referral from a past customer." Notes matter later when you look back.

Pause the video. Grab last week's sales, order count, and direct expenses. Fill in one row in your Simple Tracker. Resume when done.

Good. Now the second thing we do in this lesson: link the profit-per-offer calculation from Module 1 to this week.

From your Offer Builder worksheet, every offer you ran has a profit-per-offer-sold number. Let's say you ran the Hoodie Bundle and you made 35k profit on each of those. In the sample week, if 9 of the 21 orders were bundle orders, then 9 x 35k = 315k extra profit directly from that offer. In your tracker notes or on a separate line, record it. This is how you know which offers are actually producing money instead of just producing orders.

Third and final thing. The 10-minute weekly review. Every Sunday, after filling in the row, ask three questions out loud or write them down.

One. Did I make more or less than last week? If more --- great. What caused it? If less --- is it a seasonal slow week, or did something break?

Two. Did the offer I ran this week work? Check the profit-per-offer line. Decide: keep running, adjust the price or channel, or drop it.

Three. What one thing should I change next week? Not 10 things. One. Maybe: "Run the bundle again but promote it more through the WA group instead of Stories." Or: "Cut the free delivery on the bundle, margin was too thin." Or: "Send the re-engagement broadcast to 90+ customers on Tuesday."

That's the whole review. Two minutes filling in. Ten minutes thinking and one decision. Combined with the Money Separation habit from Lesson 4.1, this is enough to make consistent, non-random improvements every single week.

Numbers module complete. We now have the four core skills: offers, buying experience, customers and follow-up, and simple weekly tracking. The final module --- the 90-Day Roadmap --- ties all four together into a specific Week 1 to Week 12 plan, and then shows you how to review at Day 90.

### 5. Exact script

This is the final lesson of the course.

You've done 12 weeks. Offers, buying experience, customers and follow-up, weekly numbers. This week, fill in the Day 90 Review sheet. We are going to walk through the six questions together. Have Page 2 of the 90-Day Plan open.

These six questions are the same questions I would ask you if you and I were sitting down for a real business review. There are no wrong answers. Write the truth.

One. Which offers made me the most money this quarter?

Look back at the profit-per-offer rows from the Offer Builder and the offer-run lines from the Simple Tracker every week. Rank the three offers by profit produced, not units sold. Write the winner, the second, the third.

Two. Which products sold best, and which didn't sell well?

Hero product still winning? Did the complementary product or the entry product actually produce? Did the dead-stock bundle actually clear stock, or is there still some left? Rank the products. Write next to the bad ones: drop, or re-price, or test a smaller quantity next time.

Three. How many new customers did I keep? (Repeat buyers.)

Look at your customer list. Count the number of people who bought more than once. That number, divided by total customers, is your rough repeat-purchase rate. Write the percentage. If it is under 15% --- Module 3 needs more of your time next quarter. If it's 25% or higher, you're already doing retention very well.

Four. Has my average order value gone up compared to 90 days ago?

Use the tracker. Pull the earliest week's AOV, pull the most recent week's AOV. If it's up, which offers or add-ons are causing the increase? Double those. If it's flat, the offers are not reaching enough people or they are not attractive enough.

Five. What one thing should I double down on next quarter?

Not three things. Not "improve everything." One thing. The single lever, offer type, channel, or product that produced the best return. Write it. This becomes the anchor of the next 90 days.

Six. Is my current system starting to feel like too much manual work?

This is a yes or no question. Answer honestly. If filling in the Simple Tracker each week, managing the customer list, and running the broadcasts is starting to take hours instead of minutes --- not because you're slow, but because the order volume and product range grew --- this is the DIY ceiling.

If your answer to question six was YES --- the manual system is cracking under real volume --- then the next natural step is not a bigger spreadsheet and a bigger notebook. The next step is a proper revenue system. A website that routes orders, a dashboard that automatically shows sales, products, customers, segments, and trends, and later automated WhatsApp follow-up and recovery.
