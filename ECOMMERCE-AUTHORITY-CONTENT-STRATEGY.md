## PART 1: POSITIONING & PRINCIPLES

### Audience
- **Primary:** Existing African business owners selling products (fashion, cosmetics, electronics, food, home goods, fitness equipment, decor).
- They already sell through physical shops, Instagram, WhatsApp, TikTok, referrals, or other channels.
- They already have customers, inquiries, products, and some revenue.
- **NOT targeting:** Beginners, people who haven't started a business, people looking for "how to start" content, people with no existing customers or sales.

### Language Rules
- Use business-owner language: "selling online," "Instagram sales," "WhatsApp orders," "online customers," "product sales," "customers," "sales," "offers," "orders," "products."
- "E-commerce" is acceptable when it makes sense, but do NOT overuse it. Most African business owners don't call themselves "e-commerce businesses." They say "I sell clothes online" or "I run a product business."
- Titles should immediately make sense to someone already running a business.

### Purpose of Public Content
- NOT to repeat the private VSL. The VSL explains the offer, features, benefits, pricing, and how I help.
- Public content should: warm people up, build authority, break limiting beliefs, educate about problems they may not realize they have, show opportunities, and make them see the value of having a better revenue system.
- The viewer should finish thinking: "This person understands my business," "I didn't realize this was costing me sales," "I need to fix this," or "I need to understand this better."

### Credibility Sources (use only these --- never fabricate)
- Experience studying successful e-commerce businesses internationally (Shopify-style systems).
- Building websites and systems for real product businesses.
- Currently studying, building software/business systems, and learning AI while living in China.
- Working with real businesses (Darro clothing brand, mum's school).
- Demonstrated knowledge through teardowns, analysis, examples, screenshots, and actual work.
- NEVER invent clients, results, revenue numbers, testimonials, or case studies.

### Tone
- Intelligent business analysis, not a sales pitch.
- Speak as someone who already understands the world of business owners.
- Use problems, numbers, decisions, customer behavior, sales, products, offers, margins, tracking, conversion, follow-up, and growth naturally.
- Do NOT sound like a beginner tutorial or a motivational speaker.
- Calm, analytical, authoritative. Like a consultant who's studied the problem deeply.

### The Underlying Journey
```
ATTENTION -> CONVERSION -> MEASUREMENT -> REVENUE GROWTH
```
- Video 1 addresses: ATTENTION (where it leaks)
- Video 2 addresses: CONVERSION (what makes it work)
- Video 3 addresses: MEASUREMENT (why you can't see it)
- Video 4 addresses: REVENUE GROWTH (how to grow from what you have)

## PART 3: THE FOUR-VIDEO FRAMEWORK

### Overview

**Video 1**
- Core title: Why Some Businesses Sell More Without Getting More Followers
- Core angle: Hidden sales losses. The weak journey from attention to purchase.
- Belief challenged: "I need more followers / more content"
- Journey stage: Attention to Conversion
- Default CTA: Revenue Leak Audit (plus a very brief Apply mention at the very end for clearly established viewers)

**Video 2**
- Core title: What Makes Someone Actually Buy From Your Business Online?
- Core angle: What strong buying experiences do differently (no website assumed).
- Belief challenged: "I just need to post products or build a page"
- Journey stage: Conversion
- Default CTA: Revenue Leak Audit only

**Video 3**
- Core title: 7 Numbers Every Product Business Owner Should Know (Ranked By Money Impact)
- Core angle: The 7 metrics that directly put more money in (or keep money from leaking out of) a product business. Ranked in priority order: Make more money first, Save money second, Save time last. Includes debunking of common vanity-metric lies (total revenue is not #1, followers/likes don't pay bills). Practical starter approach: notebook + one Sheet.
- Belief challenged: "I know my business well enough / I can feel what's working" and "Total sales revenue is my most important number"
- Journey stage: Measurement
- Default CTA: Revenue Leak Audit (plus Apply at end --- wanting real numbers naturally leads to a dashboard)
- 7-Metric priority order used in the video script and in the audit:
  Priority Tier 1: MAKE MORE MONEY (highest impact, fix first)
  1. Gross Profit Margin per product (the ceiling of every other metric)
  2. Inquiry/Visit to Order Conversion Rate (smallest lift = biggest revenue multiplier)
  3. Average Order Value (AOV) (fastest lever to move WITHOUT more customers)
  Priority Tier 2: SAVE MONEY / STOP WASTE (second priority)
  4. Repeat Purchase Rate (cheapest customer you already have)
  5. Revenue per Visitor (RPV) --- catches the trade-offs that CVR or AOV alone hide
  6. Traffic Source by Actual Revenue (not clicks or likes --- which channel actually brought paying customers --- kill spend on dead channels)
  Priority Tier 3: SAVE TIME (important, but only after money metrics are handled)
  7. Funnel Drop-off Point (exactly WHERE customers disappear --- PDP? cart? checkout? --- tells you the single fix to do next)
- Vanity metric lies (used as the "belief challenge" section in the video):
  Lie 1: "Total Revenue is my #1 metric." No. A business can grow revenue 30% and be LESS profitable. Revenue is a lagging indicator. Gross margin is the ceiling.
  Lie 2: "Followers, likes, views matter." No correlation to revenue. A page with 100k followers and a 0.2% conversion rate is worse than a page with 5k followers and 4% conversion.
  Lie 3: "My best-selling product (most units) is my most important product." Usually the opposite. Your highest-volume product is often your lowest-margin one.
  Lie 4: "I need more customers first." No. Most businesses have 2-3x more revenue available from converting the traffic they already have before acquiring one more new customer.
  Lie 5: "Posting more content fixes slow sales." If conversion is 2%, posting to twice as many people just doubles your work for 2 extra sales. Fix conversion first. Then posting actually works.

**Video 4**
- Core title: More Customers Isn't Always the Answer: 6 Offers That Grow a Product Business Without New Customers
- Core angle: 6 offer structures, ranked by how much revenue they typically generate from existing demand (no new customers needed). Each offer is explained simply --- definition first, then how it improves revenue in plain terms, with a big-brand example ONLY where it aids understanding (not every offer needs one). Do NOT over-explain. Only Offer 1 (bundles) has a confirmed Darro example (World Cup + new season kits bundle, no prices, never call it BOGO). No invented Darro prices anywhere. Includes debunking: "offers are not discounts."
- Belief challenged: "I need more customers to grow" and "better offers = just lower your prices"
- Journey stage: Revenue Growth
- Default CTA: Website + dashboard as the best way to run and test multiple offers, leading into Apply for established businesses. No Revenue Leak Audit for this video.
- 6 Offer Type priority order (ranked by revenue impact, largest first):
  1. Product Bundles: highest AOV lift 20-40% per order. Gymshark reference: Buy 2 Get 20% off bundles. Darro reference: leftover World Cup + new season kits (confirmed, no prices).
  2. Quantity and Tiered Offers: Buy 2 Save 15%, Buy 3 Save 25%. Works where customers genuinely buy more than one. Reliable AOV lift.
  3. Free Shipping Thresholds: set a bit above current AOV. Gymshark reference: free-shipping threshold (verify current amount on their site before recording).
  4. Checkout Add-ons and Upsells: small item at checkout. 10-25% lift on affected orders with zero extra acquisition cost.
  5. Entry and Hero Product Pair: linked pair. Entry product gets the customer into the list, Hero product monetizes the relationship long-term. Repeat revenue impact over 3-12 months.
  6. Genuine Scarcity and Limited Drops: improves conversion on the drop window, not AOV. Smaller but fast impact, builds story/engagement.
- "These are not discounts" principle: Before running any offer, write out cost price / offer price / profit per unit. If offer profit per transaction is NOT bigger than single-sale profit, don't run it. Profit-first math, not hope-based volume.

Titles can be refined but the four strategic angles above must remain distinct.

### MANDATORY: Standard Video Opening Framework (all 4 public videos, every time)

Every long-form YouTube video must open with the same 3-part structure, in this exact order, in the first 45-75 seconds. This is how the viewer knows immediately: what is this about, why should I trust this person, and exactly what am I getting if I stay. The opening is NOT a CTA. It is a promise of the value they will receive in this video. The 3 parts are:

1. PROBLEM (5-10 seconds). State the EXACT pain or question this viewer has right now, in simple language. No metaphors. No hook questions they can answer "no" to. Example: "If you own a product business and you work hard every month but the money at the end never matches the effort, this video is for you."
2. CREDIBILITY (10-30 seconds). Why I am the person to teach this specific topic. Must be real, specific, and give context: who I am, why this matters to me, and how I know what I am about to share. Never generic. Example: "I run a clothing brand called Darro. I spent over a year studying how the biggest Shopify brands grow, built my own measurement systems, and tested every structure on my own business."
3. EXPECTATIONS (15-25 seconds). 3 specific, benefit-aligned promises of what I am going to give them in this video. Each promise answers "how does this move their business forward." Standard pattern for the 3 promises: "In the next [X] minutes I'm going to show you: (a) the real [problems/leaks/numbers/offers] that most owners miss, (b) exactly how each one BENEFITS you or costs you money if you ignore it, and (c) one simple thing you can do this week to put it into practice." The 3 promises must be concrete and benefit-led, not topic-led. Do NOT name the CTA. Do NOT say "stick around" or "subscribe" in the opening.

This 3-part structure replaces all generic hook questions. It gets the right person to stay because it identifies them directly, confirms relevance, and removes guesswork about what the video contains.

### Script Writing Rules (all public videos)

1. BENEFIT FIRST, ALWAYS. Every section, every metric, every offer opens with what it does for the viewer before explaining what it is. The viewer hears the value before they see any visual or hear any definition.

2. NO "THIS COSTS YOU" ENDINGS. Do not tell the viewer how much a lie or mistake costs. Instead, explain what is actually happening and let the consequence emerge naturally from the explanation. The viewer should understand the cost through the story, not be told it.

3. EXPLAIN LIKE TALKING TO A BEGINNER. Use simple, everyday language. Most of the audience does not know software, systems, or metric names. If a term needs explaining and is essential, explain it in plain words. If it can be skipped, skip it. Common e-commerce terms (stock, orders, customers, delivery) are fine.

4. NO TIER OR NUMBER ANNOUNCEMENTS IN THE SPOKEN SCRIPT. The tier structure (make money first, save money second, save time last) is used internally to organize what comes first. Do NOT say "Tier 1, Number 2" or "Number three, Tier one" on camera. The viewer does not need to hear the ranking system.

5. NO "THIS SUNDAY" OR TIME-SPECIFIC ACTION CALLS. Do not tell the viewer when to act. Give them the information and let them decide. Instead of "This Sunday: do X," simply explain how to find the number or what to look at. Make it general.

6. NO REPETITIVE PATTERNS. Vary the structure of each section. Do not use the same sentence pattern for every metric or offer. Make it sound human and conversational, not formulaic.

7. LIES FOLLOW THIS PATTERN: (a) State the belief most people have. (b) Explain WHY they think it is important, their reasoning. (c) Show what is actually happening, the reality. (d) Let the consequence emerge from the explanation. Do not say "this costs you X."

8. METRICS FOLLOW THIS PATTERN: (a) Open with the benefit, what it does for them. (b) Explain what the number is in plain language. (c) Give context or benchmark without jargon. (d) Explain how to find it, in general terms, no time-specific call to action.

9. CANVA TITLE PATTERN (for every listed point, lie or metric). Every time you introduce a new numbered point: (a) say a short transition phrase ("Next.", "Third one.", etc.), then (b) SAY THE TITLE OUT LOUD in plain words that exactly match the Canva card text, then (c) cut to the Canva title card on screen, then (d) explain. The viewer hears the title from your mouth before it appears as text. This is the rhythm for every single point. Do not skip the spoken title or the Canva card will appear disconnected from what you are saying. Transition phrases should vary naturally --- do not use the exact same phrase every time (alternate: "Next.", "Third.", "Number four.", "Last one.", "First up.", etc.).

### How survey data feeds content

The **Beacon agency website** (NOT the e-commerce demo or Darro clothing brand) includes a Revenue Leak Audit survey/quiz that serves as a lead magnet. When business owners complete it, their answers reveal:
- Their biggest current challenge (lost sales, low conversion, no data, customer retention)

- Their average order value and order volume
- Whether they have follow-up systems
- Whether they track any business numbers
- What they think their problem is vs. what actually might be

1. **Identify patterns:** If 60% of respondents say "my biggest problem is getting more followers" but their data shows 2% conversion --- the content needs to address the real problem (conversion) while acknowledging their stated belief.

3. **Use real language:** Survey responses tell you the exact words business owners use. Use those words in scripts and titles.

4. **Double down on what resonates:** If a specific concept in a video gets the most engagement, survey responses, or audit requests --- make more content around that concept.

## PART 9: CTA SYSTEM --- TWO-CTA FRAMEWORK

There are exactly TWO CTAs in public YouTube authority content. They are never presented the same way, never used interchangeably, and never both pushed hard in the same video.

---

### CTA 1: Revenue Leak Audit (PRIMARY, used in every video)

**What it is:**
- A free resource / lead-generation tool.
- Gives personalized guidance to business owners about where their business may be losing revenue.
- NOT a sales pitch for the agency.
- Lives on the **Beacon agency website** (not e-commerce demo, not Darro).

**How to mention it in the video:**

Do NOT wait until the final 10 seconds. Mention it NATURALLY INSIDE the video when the topic directly connects to a problem the audit helps diagnose. The wording should feel like part of the teaching, not an advertisement.

**Examples of natural insertion:**

- When walking through the 5 customer-journey leaks (Video 1):
  > "If you're dealing with this in your own business, I actually put together a free Revenue Leak Audit that walks you through each of these areas and shows you where I'd start improving. You can find it below, and it'll give you a personalized view of where the biggest opportunities are for your specific business."

  > "The worst part is most owners can't see where people are dropping off --- they just see fewer sales. If you want help identifying the exact area your buying experience is weakest, the Revenue Leak Audit covers all of this. Link is below."

- When listing the 7 numbers owners should know (Video 3):
  > "If you couldn't answer most of these, I built a free Revenue Leak Audit that goes through each of these seven numbers and tells you how many you're already tracking and what to put in place first. It's below."

- When explaining how most businesses have weak offers (Video 4):
  > "Running one offer manually is fine. Running three or four at the same time and figuring out which one actually made you the most profit? That is hard without a system. A website with a dashboard lets you put all your offers in one place and see exactly which one produces which profit. If you are established and ready for that, there is an application link below."

**Optional: show a quick example of audit output**

Where useful, briefly show ONE example of a finding/recommendation from the audit:
> "For example, if your biggest gap is conversion, the audit might tell you something like: '80% of your inquiring customers never receive a second follow-up message --- implementing a simple two-step follow-up is the first fix.' Just one example. It's different for every business. The audit gives you your own version."

Do NOT show the entire audit. Give one example that teases the value.

### CTA 2: Apply to Work With Us (SELECTIVE, not every video)

**What it is:**
- Application form for established businesses that are clearly a good fit for the agency's higher-value service.
- Application qualifies people into either a starter path or a high-value path.
- NOT for beginners, startups, or businesses that are clearly too early.

**Qualification implications (language audience understands):**
The Apply CTA should naturally imply the business already has:
- A real, operating product or service business.
- Existing customers, orders, or inquiries --- some level of real demand.
- Real activity such that a website, dashboard, or revenue system would actually be useful.
- Seriousness about improving how the business sells and operates.
- Willingness to invest in implementation if the solution fits.

Do NOT use marketing jargon ("high-ticket," "ROI," "qualified lead," "sales funnel"). Keep it simple for an African business owner.

**When to use it:**
Use the Apply CTA ONLY when the video's topic naturally creates a reason for an established business to consider hiring someone.

Apply-CTA guidance per video:

- **Video 1 (Lost Sales):** At very end, briefly. Reason: viewers who "get" the leaks problem are often already running real operations, so a system conversation makes sense.
- **Video 2 (Conversion):** Skip or extremely brief only. Reason: conversion fixes are largely DIY-able so the fit is weak.
- **Video 3 (7 Numbers):** Yes, at end. Reason: seeing numbers naturally leads viewers to want a real dashboard, which is agency work.
- **Video 4 (Offers):** Yes, at end. Reason: offers combined with product/customer systems naturally lead to full revenue-system implementation, which is agency work.

**How to mention it:**

Position it around FIT, not desperation. Not "hire me." Not "apply now." A genuine statement about who the service is actually for:

> "And if you're already running a business --- you're getting customers, you're getting inquiries, products are selling --- and you're at the point where you want a proper system built around your business instead of trying to piece everything together yourself, there's also an application below. That's for businesses that are already operating and want us to look at what could actually be built for them."

Or for the Numbers/Visibility video:
> "If the idea of seeing all your numbers clearly is something you want but you don't want to spend weeks building spreadsheets and dashboards yourself --- and you already have enough orders and customers that it matters --- there's an application below. That's the direct work we do for established businesses."

**Never use the Apply CTA in the first 80% of the video.** Keep it for the end, and keep it brief --- one sentence, maybe two.

### CTA Selection --- The 5-Question Framework (use for every future video)

Before writing any new YouTube video script, answer these five questions:

1. **What belief or problem are we addressing?**
2. **Why does an existing business owner actually care about this?**
3. **What useful insight are we teaching them?**
4. **Which part of my ecosystem does this naturally lead to?**
   - (a) Understand the problem deeper -> Revenue Leak Audit
   - (b) Want implementation -> Apply to Work With Us
   - (c) Watch the next video in the sequence only
   - (d) No CTA --- pure authority building
5. **Which CTA fits best? Revenue Leak Audit, Apply, another video, or no CTA?**

Never force a CTA simply because there must be one. The goal is not to make every viewer take action --- the goal is to move the RIGHT viewer one step closer:

```
Understand the problem -> Trust me -> Diagnose their business (Audit) -> Apply if they are ready.
```

## PART 10: SHORT-FORM DERIVATION RULES + CLIP EXTRACTION BANK

Each long-form YouTube video should produce 3-5 short-form clips (IG/TikTok).

### Rules:
- Clips must stand alone --- no "in my full video I said..."
- Each clip = ONE idea, 15-45 seconds
- Hooks must work without context
- CTA: "Full breakdown on YouTube --- link in bio" (max 1 in 3 clips)
- 2 of 3 clips should have NO CTA --- let curiosity build
- Burn-in subtitles on every clip
- Vertical 9:16 format
- Clip tone = top-of-funnel: speaks to the business owner's pain or curiosity, NOT direct selling or implementation-heavy details. The job of the clip is to make the right person stop scrolling and identify themselves as someone who has this problem.
