No new product announcement to them. A repeat customer has to start the entire conversation process all over again, just like a stranger.

Ceiling five: Decisions are memory-based, not data-based. "This product is selling well" means you remember packing a lot of it. "This week was slower" means it felt slower. There's no way to compare this month to last month, to know which product actually makes you the most profit, to know which post actually produced the orders.

Same audience. Double the sales. No extra spending, no extra content. Just more of the people who already wanted your product actually paying.

That means for every 100 people who reach out, 98 walk away without buying. Fixing even a small part of why they leave can change your revenue without spending a single shilling more on marketing.

To find your number, count how many messages or inquiries you got last week. Count how many orders you got. Orders divided by messages. That is your conversion rate.

Third. Average spend per order.

[CANVA TITLE: 3. Average Spend Per Order]

This one is the fastest way to make more money from the exact same number of customers you already have.

It is how much the average customer spends each time they buy from you. Not per month, per order.

Here is why it matters. If you find a way to make each customer spend 20 percent more per order, that is 20 percent more revenue from the same person. No new customer. No new marketing. Just a different way of presenting what you sell.

For context, the average fashion brand gets about 80 dollars per order, which is roughly 300,000 shillings. But most African product businesses are sitting much lower and do not realize it.

To find yours, take your total sales last week and divide by the number of orders. That is your average. The next video in this series is all about how to move this number up, with real examples.

Number four. Repeat customer rate.

[CANVA TITLE: 4. Repeat Customer Rate]

This next number shows you something most owners never think about. It can give you a month where a quarter of your sales come in without you posting or paying for anything.

It is your repeat customer rate. How many of the people who bought from you this month had bought from you before.

Here is why this matters. A customer who already trusts you is the cheapest customer you will ever get. You do not need to find them. You do not need to convince them. You just need to treat them well enough to come back. On top of that, repeat buyers tend to spend more than first-time buyers because they already trust your quality.

If every sale this month came from a brand new person, you are running on a treadmill. Every month starts from zero. But if 25 percent or more of your sales come from people who already bought before, the business gets easier every month because a quarter of the work is already done.

To find this, look at your last month's orders. How many of those names do you recognize? If almost none, that is the problem. And just noticing it is where fixing it starts.

Fifth. Revenue per message.

[CANVA TITLE: 5. Revenue Per Message]

This one catches something that fools you into thinking you are winning when you are actually losing money.

It is a simple number. Your total revenue divided by your total messages or visits. One number that tells you if the whole system is working.

Here is why you need it. You can convince more people to buy, but if each one spends less, you can actually make less money overall. Each number on its own looks fine. More buyers. Smaller orders. Feels like progress. But together, you lost money. This number catches that because it combines everything into one figure.

If you made 2 million from 500 messages last week, your number is 4,000 per message. Next week you made 2.2 million from 700 messages. Your number is now about 3,100 per message. Revenue went up. But you are actually earning less from every person who reaches out. Without this number, you would think you are growing.

To find it, take your total revenue last week and divide by how many people reached out or visited. Write it down. Track it for a few weeks and you will see patterns you cannot see any other way.

Six. Where paying customers come from.

[CANVA TITLE: 6. Where Paying Customers Come From]

This one tells you exactly which platform to stop wasting your time on.

It is tracking where your paying customers actually come from. Not where your likes come from. Not where your views come from. Where your money comes from.

Here is an example. Instagram brings you 100 inquiries and 10 sales. TikTok brings you 50 inquiries and 20 sales. TikTok brought you double the customers with half the audience. But most owners look at the 100 inquiries on Instagram and think that is where they should focus. They double down on the platform that gets attention, not the one that gets orders.

This is the fastest way to stop wasting hours posting on platforms that look busy but produce no money.

To find this, for every new order, just ask one question. "How did you find us?" Write the answer next to the sale. Do that for a week and you will have a clear picture of where your real customers come from.

Last one. Number seven. Where customers drop off.

[CANVA TITLE: 7. Where Customers Drop Off]

This last one saves you from testing 10 random things and guessing which one worked. It tells you the one thing to fix.

It is about understanding where in your buying process most people give up. Think about the journey a customer goes through. They see your product. They ask about it. They decide to buy. They confirm. They pay. At which step do most people disappear?

If half the people who ask about a product actually commit to buy, but only a small fraction finish paying, then your payment step is the problem. Fix that. If most people view your product but very few actually ask about it, then your product presentation or your price is the problem. Fix that.

This one does not directly put money in your pocket. But it saves you the 20 hours a month of testing random changes that never move anything. It tells you exactly where to focus.

To find it, go through a customer's purchase in your own business, step by step. Where did you get bored or confused? Where would you have given up? That is probably where your customers are dropping off too.

Gymshark does this --- order above a set amount and delivery is free. Customers who came to buy one thing end up adding a small item so they do not pay for shipping.

For your business, set the threshold a bit above what your average order is now. Same customer, slightly bigger order, every time.

[CANVA TITLE: 4. Checkout Add-ons and Upsells]

[CANVA TITLE: 5. Entry and Hero Product Pair]

Two products working together. An entry product is a cheap, low-risk first purchase. It does not make you much money on its own. What it does is turn a stranger into a paying customer who now trusts you and is on your list.

Weeks or months later, that same person comes back and buys your hero product at full price. The hero carries the real margin. The entry product's job was to start the relationship.

Most successful online brands are built around a pair like this.

Last one. Number six. Genuine scarcity and limited drops.

[CANVA TITLE: 6. Genuine Scarcity and Limited Drops]

A drop is when you release a limited quantity of a product for a short time. Customers who were going to "buy next week" buy today because the stock is about to run out.

This does not increase how much each customer spends. It improves conversion during the drop window. Faster cash in the bank. But it is not a permanent structure, so it is last.

The one rule. Scarcity only works when it is real. Fake "only 2 left" on stock you have plenty of destroys trust. Trust is worth more than one extra week of sales.
